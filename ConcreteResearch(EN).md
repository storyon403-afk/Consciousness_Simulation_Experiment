## Ⅰ. The Presence, Absence, and Transitions of Consciousness (01–09)

> Ongoing neural activity, behavioral responses, memory, and experience are distinct measures.

### 01. Alternating Between Sleep and Wakefulness

- **Scenario**: You closed your eyes last night and woke up this morning; the interval feels almost instantaneous.
- **Observed findings**: Sleep includes distinct, measurable physiological stages; subjective time need not match clock time.
- **Question to explore**: Does an interval you cannot remember necessarily contain no experience?
- **Research link**: [NIH: Stages of Sleep](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/sleep/stages-of-sleep)

### 02. Consciousness in Dreams

- **Scenario**: In a dream, you run, feel afraid, and even hold conversations. Only after waking do you realize that the scene did not exist.
- **Observed findings**: People can report sleep experiences disconnected from their immediate external surroundings.
- **Question to explore**: Must a subjective world depend on sensory input from the external world in real time?
- **Research link**: [Review of Anesthesia and Consciousness: Connected and Disconnected Consciousness](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8437133/)

### 03. Lucid Dreaming

- **Scenario**: You suddenly realize that you are dreaming and try to move your eyes deliberately.
- **Observed findings**: Lucid dreamers can communicate with sleep researchers using eye-movement signals agreed upon in advance.
- **Question to explore**: What conditions are needed to recognize “I am having an experience” within a dream?
- **Research link**: [The Cognitive Neuroscience of Lucid Dreaming](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6451677/)

### 04. Changes in Consciousness Under Anesthesia

- **Scenario**: After surgery, you remember nothing of the procedure, but this does not establish that you had no experiences during it.
- **Observed findings**: Anesthesia can produce unresponsiveness, amnesia, and altered states of consciousness; these measures do not always coincide.
- **Question to explore**: How can loss of consciousness be distinguished from failure to form memories?
- **Research link**: [Anesthesia and the Neurobiology of Consciousness](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11098701/)

### 05. Coma and Severe Disorders of Consciousness

- **Scenario**: A patient does not open their eyes or respond normally, and you cannot directly ask what they are experiencing.
- **Observed findings**: Severe brain injury can substantially impair arousal and behavioral responsiveness; bedside assessments and brain-activity tests still have limitations.
- **Question to explore**: Without a channel for reporting experience, how can consciousness be assessed cautiously?
- **Research link**: [NEJM 2024: Disorders of Consciousness and Covert Cognition](https://www.nejm.org/doi/10.1056/NEJMoa2400645)

### 06. Covert Cognition / Cognitive Motor Dissociation

- **Scenario**: You ask a patient who shows no visible response to imagine an action, and instruments detect brain activity consistent with the instruction.
- **Observed findings**: In a 2024 multicenter study, 60 of 241 patients unable to follow commands behaviorally showed evidence of this kind.
- **Question to explore**: Can behavioral unresponsiveness be treated as evidence of an absence of consciousness?
- **Research link**: [NEJM Multicenter Study](https://www.nejm.org/doi/10.1056/NEJMoa2400645)

### 07. Locked-in Syndrome

- **Scenario**: You understand your family's speech and can think clearly, yet can hardly move or make a sound.
- **Observed findings**: Certain brainstem injuries can cause profound paralysis and loss of speech output while preserving consciousness.
- **Question to explore**: To what extent can consciousness be separated from the body's channels of expression?
- **Research link**: [Clinical Review of Locked-in Syndrome](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC549115/)

### 08. Brain Death

- **Scenario**: Machines can temporarily support circulation, but clinicians must follow a strict procedure to determine whether brain function as a whole has been permanently lost.
- **Observed findings**: Brain death has explicit clinical diagnostic criteria and must not be confused with coma or unresponsive wakefulness syndrome.
- **Question to explore**: What irreversible changes have occurred in the physical functions that support human consciousness?
- **Research link**: [2023 Guideline for Determining Brain Death / Death by Neurologic Criteria](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10791061/)

### 09. Partial Recovery After a Disorder of Consciousness

- **Scenario**: A patient who has long been unresponsive gradually shows purposeful movements during repeated assessments.
- **Observed findings**: Some patients with disorders of consciousness may regain certain behavioral signs of consciousness over long-term follow-up, with substantial individual variation.
- **Question to explore**: Does recovery involve arousal, access to information, the ability to express oneself, or several of these together?
- **Research link**: [Longitudinal Study of Disorders of Consciousness](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6924536/)

## Ⅱ. Sensation, Perception, and Conscious Experience (10–18)

> Signals reaching the eyes or other senses do not necessarily all become subjective experience.

### 10. Sensory Adaptation

- **Scenario**: A strong smell strikes you when you enter a room, but after ten or fifteen minutes you barely notice it.
- **Observed findings**: Perceived intensity and neural sensitivity may change during sustained stimulation.
- **Question to explore**: Why can a continuously present signal fade out of subjective experience?
- **Research link**: [Review of Visual Adaptation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4742349/)

### 11. Perceptual Illusions

- **Scenario**: The same ambiguous image looks like a vase one moment and faces the next.
- **Observed findings**: Perceptual interpretation can change even when the physical stimulus remains constant.
- **Question to explore**: Does perception read reality directly, or construct an interpretation constrained by it?
- **Research link**: [Review of Perceptual Rivalry Across Species](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7541519/)

### 12. Blindsight

- **Scenario**: You say, “I did not see anything on the left,” yet repeatedly guess its location at better-than-chance rates.
- **Observed findings**: Some patients with damage to the primary visual cortex (V1) retain visual discrimination despite impaired normal subjective visual experience; the precise characterization of its different forms remains debated.
- **Question to explore**: Why does information processing not always come with a clear sense of seeing?
- **Research link**: [The Nature of Blindsight and Theories of Consciousness](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8884361/)

### 13. Inattentional Blindness

- **Scenario**: While concentrating on counting passes of a ball, you fail to notice a conspicuous “gorilla” walking through the scene.
- **Observed findings**: Attentional tasks can substantially influence reports of unexpected events.
- **Question to explore**: Why might a signal that clearly reaches the eyes fail to enter current consciousness?
- **Research link**: [The Classic Simons and Chabris Experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10694957/)

### 14. Change Blindness

- **Scenario**: Two photographs alternate repeatedly. A large object disappears from the scene, but it takes you a long time to notice.
- **Observed findings**: A brief visual interruption can make substantial visual changes difficult to detect.
- **Question to explore**: Do we really maintain a complete internal representation of the world before our eyes?
- **Research link**: [Rensink et al., 1997](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/j.1467-9280.1997.tb00427.x/)

### 15. Binocular Rivalry

- **Scenario**: Each eye sees a different image, and your experience spontaneously alternates between them.
- **Observed findings**: Even with constant input, the dominant percept can switch periodically.
- **Question to explore**: Is subjective content determined by the stimulus, or by internal competition and feedback?
- **Research link**: [Seeing the Invisible: Binocular Rivalry](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2689366/)

### 16. Internal Experience Disconnected from the External World

- **Scenario**: While daydreaming, you suddenly become absorbed in an imagined conversation that never took place.
- **Observed findings**: Spontaneous thought, dreams, and some anesthetic states can contain internal experiences without perception of the current external world.
- **Question to explore**: Is real-time external input a necessary condition for consciousness?
- **Research link**: [Review of Internal and External Consciousness Under Anesthesia](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10539891/)

### 17. Phantom Limbs

- **Scenario**: Your arm has been amputated, yet you can still feel it curled up or even itching.
- **Observed findings**: Sensations or pain in an absent limb may persist; proposed mechanisms include both peripheral and central processes.
- **Question to explore**: Is bodily experience identical to the body's current physical form?
- **Research link**: [Review of Phantom Limb Pain Research](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9304093/)

### 18. The Rubber Hand Illusion

- **Scenario**: You watch a fake hand being touched in synchrony with your real hand and gradually feel that the fake hand “is mine.”
- **Observed findings**: Synchronized visual, tactile, and other signals can manipulate the sense of body ownership.
- **Question to explore**: How is the experience “this is my body” constructed?
- **Research link**: [Systematic Review of the Rubber Hand Illusion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8413782/)

## Ⅲ. Memory and the Continuity of Consciousness (19–25)

> Memory changes who we are, but this does not mean that every conscious moment requires an intact store of long-term memories.

### 19. Anterograde Amnesia

- **Scenario**: You have just finished a conversation, but a little later cannot remember that the other person visited; you were still able to converse normally at the time.
- **Observed findings**: Damage to the medial temporal lobe can severely disrupt the formation of new declarative long-term memories without necessarily eliminating consciousness in the moment.
- **Question to explore**: Does remaining conscious necessarily require retaining long-term memories?
- **Research link**: [The Case of H.M. and Dissociations in Memory](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2888763/)

### 20. Preserved Procedural Learning

- **Scenario**: You forget having practiced mirror drawing, yet your hand becomes more skilled each day.
- **Observed findings**: Patients such as H.M. can retain certain forms of skill learning despite being unable to explicitly recall the training.
- **Question to explore**: Are learning, remembering that you learned, and being aware that you are learning the same function?
- **Research link**: [H.M.'s Mirror-Drawing Learning](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2888763/)

### 21. Cue-Triggered Autobiographical Memory

- **Scenario**: The smell of a particular laundry detergent suddenly brings back an afternoon from your childhood.
- **Observed findings**: Cues such as odors can trigger autobiographical memories accompanied by vivid emotional experiences.
- **Question to explore**: In what form does a memory exist when it is not being recalled?
- **Research link**: [Review of Odor-Evoked Memories](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5039451/)

### 22. False Memories and Memory Reconstruction

- **Scenario**: You feel certain that something happened in your childhood, then discover that the memory may have come from someone else's account.
- **Observed findings**: Experiments can induce false autobiographical recollections; remembering is not a reliable replay of events exactly as they occurred.
- **Question to explore**: To what extent does a sense of personal continuity depend on reconstructing memories?
- **Research link**: [Meta-Analysis of Implanting Rich False Autobiographical Memories](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10126919/)

### 23. Optogenetic Manipulation of Memory

- **Scenario**: A mouse freezes in a place where it has never received an electric shock because a specific population of memory-related neurons has been activated.
- **Observed findings**: A 2013 study used optogenetics to establish a false contextual fear-memory association; this is not equivalent to directly reading the mouse's subjective experience.
- **Question to explore**: What causal role do specific neuronal populations play in the behavioral expression of memory?
- **Research link**: [Science: Creating a False Memory](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23888038/)

### 24. Spontaneous Memory Replay

- **Scenario**: After practicing a route during the day, related sequences of neuronal activity reappear during rest.
- **Observed findings**: Hippocampal replay can be observed during rest, sleep, and wakefulness and serves more than one function.
- **Question to explore**: Are memories still being processed when we are not consciously trying to recall them?
- **Research link**: [Hippocampal Replay During Wakefulness and Sleep](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7898724/)

### 25. Memory Consolidation and Plasticity

- **Scenario**: An experience becomes more stable or changes during subsequent sleep, retrieval, and new learning.
- **Observed findings**: Memory engrams can be reactivated and reorganized, in association with multiple mechanisms of plasticity.
- **Question to explore**: Is “preserving the past” actually a continuously changing process?
- **Research link**: [Memory Engram Stability and Flexibility](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41386-024-01979-z)

## Ⅳ. Automatic Processing and Hierarchical Control (26–30)

> The body can perform actions without step-by-step conscious monitoring; subjective intention cannot always direct movement either.

### 26. Reflex Activity

- **Scenario**: Your hand touches a scalding object and quickly withdraws before you have clearly thought about it.
- **Observed findings**: Some protective actions can be organized by spinal and brainstem pathways; nociceptive processing is not the same as subjective pain.
- **Question to explore**: Does behavior that prevents injury require consciousness?
- **Research link**: [IASP: Distinguishing Nociception from Pain](https://www.iasp-pain.org/resources/terminology/)

### 27. Automatic Motor Control

- **Scenario**: Once you are skilled at cycling, you can think about other things without directing each muscle individually.
- **Observed findings**: Practice makes motor control more automatic and is accompanied by changes in cortical and basal ganglia network activity.
- **Question to explore**: How are execution, intention, and conscious monitoring divided among different processes?
- **Research link**: [Neural Mechanisms of Habit Learning and Automaticity](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2862890/)

### 28. Alien Hand Syndrome

- **Scenario**: Your hand grasps objects or even interferes with your other hand; you feel that these are not actions you intended.
- **Observed findings**: Certain brain lesions can cause apparently purposeful limb movements that are not under voluntary control.
- **Question to explore**: How can an action occur separately from the feeling “I initiated this action”?
- **Research link**: [Alien Hand: A 2025 Research Review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12449344/)

### 29. Split Brain

- **Scenario**: Information reaches only one cerebral hemisphere, and the patient's responses on the two sides show a dissociation.
- **Observed findings**: Severing the corpus callosum can disrupt some forms of information integration, but whether consciousness divides into two subjects remains debated.
- **Question to explore**: How much physical connectivity does subjective unity require?
- **Research link**: [Split Brain: Review of the Unity of Consciousness](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7305066/)

### 30. Sleepwalking

- **Scenario**: A person walks to the kitchen while asleep but has no clear memory of doing so the next day.
- **Observed findings**: Non-REM parasomnias can involve complex actions followed by partial or complete amnesia.
- **Question to explore**: Can complex action, arousal, and self-report be dissociated from one another?
- **Research link**: [Review of Adult Non-REM Parasomnias](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7509660/)

## Ⅴ. Emotion, Interoception, and Psychological States (31–37)

> Bodily signals, neurochemistry, and cognitive appraisal jointly influence how we feel.

### 31. Interoception

- **Scenario**: Without looking at an instrument, you can still feel your heartbeat, hunger, rapid breathing, and physical fatigue.
- **Observed findings**: The perception and interpretation of signals from inside the body are closely related to emotion.
- **Question to explore**: Does part of the sense of “I” come from within the body?
- **Research link**: [2025: Interoception and Emotion](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-psych-020924-125202)

### 32. Neurochemical Modulation of Emotion

- **Scenario**: After sleep deprivation, medication, or hormonal changes, your emotional response to the same event differs.
- **Observed findings**: Dopamine, serotonin, norepinephrine, and other substances jointly contribute to emotion, motivation, and decision-making; there is no simple one-substance-to-one-emotion correspondence.
- **Question to explore**: How do chemical modulation and brain network activity jointly shape emotional experience?
- **Research link**: [Review of Neuromodulatory Systems and Their Interactions](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5744617/)

### 33. Placebo / Nocebo Effects

- **Scenario**: You expect a treatment to relieve pain and may feel less pain even if it contains no active ingredient.
- **Observed findings**: Expectations and treatment context can modulate pain experience; a 2024 experiment in mice identified a related analgesic circuit.
- **Question to explore**: How can beliefs change experience through a physical nervous system?
- **Research link**: [Nature 2024: A Circuit for Placebo Analgesia](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07816-z)

### 34. Pain Asymbolia

- **Scenario**: You recognize that a pinprick is painful, yet feel little fear or urgency to withdraw.
- **Observed findings**: In rare cases, recognition of painful stimuli dissociates from the emotional and avoidance responses that normally accompany them.
- **Question to explore**: Can the sensory and aversive dimensions of pain be separated?
- **Research link**: [Pain Asymbolia and the Affective Dimension of Pain](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10860504/)

### 35. Stress Alters Cognition

- **Scenario**: Your heart races before an exam, familiar knowledge suddenly becomes hard to retrieve, and your judgment becomes hesitant.
- **Observed findings**: Stress and cortisol levels are associated with decision-making performance, but their effects depend on the task and individual differences.
- **Question to explore**: Why can internal physiological states change how readily cognitive resources can be accessed?
- **Research link**: [Systematic Review of Stress, Cortisol, and Decision-Making](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35589606/)

### 36. Automatic Physiological Responses Related to Emotion

- **Scenario**: You do not have time to clearly identify a briefly presented threatening face, yet your skin conductance may change.
- **Observed findings**: Some studies of masked emotional stimuli report physiological effects, but whether their processing is “entirely unconscious” remains the subject of substantial methodological debate.
- **Question to explore**: Where is the boundary between physiological alertness and consciously felt fear?
- **Research link**: [Study of the Meta-Awareness Debate in Masked-Face Experiments](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29239755/)

### 37. Pain Does Not Correspond Exactly to Tissue Damage

- **Scenario**: You may experience severe pain without obvious new injury; conversely, an injury may temporarily cause no felt pain.
- **Observed findings**: IASP explicitly distinguishes pain experience from nociception: pain is influenced by biological, psychological, and social factors.
- **Question to explore**: How does physical stimulation become the irreducible subjective experience of “pain”?
- **Research link**: [IASP's 2020 Definition of Pain](https://www.iasp-pain.org/resources/terminology/)

## Ⅵ. Physical Dynamics and Instability (38–42)

> The nervous system is not an ideal program free of noise or metabolic constraints, but complexity does not automatically amount to consciousness.

### 38. Stochasticity in Synaptic Transmission

- **Scenario**: Apparently identical neural inputs produce postsynaptic responses that differ between two transmissions.
- **Observed findings**: Neurotransmitter vesicle release is probabilistic, and synaptic responses vary.
- **Question to explore**: Is this physical instability necessary for dynamics associated with consciousness?
- **Research link**: [Experimental Study of Stochastic Synaptic Vesicle Release](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4672768/)

### 39. Spontaneous Fluctuations in Neural Activity

- **Scenario**: A person sits quietly with their eyes closed and no task input, yet their brain is far from “shut down.”
- **Observed findings**: Resting-state networks exhibit ongoing, self-organized patterns of correlated activity.
- **Question to explore**: How do internal activity and external stimuli jointly determine present experience?
- **Research link**: [Dynamical Balance in Resting Brain Networks](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4139497/)

### 40. Energy Supply Alters Cognition and Arousal

- **Scenario**: During severe hypoglycemia, you may become confused, struggle to speak, or even lose consciousness.
- **Observed findings**: The nervous system depends heavily on a continuous energy supply; metabolic disturbances can alter brain function.
- **Question to explore**: Which metabolic conditions does consciousness depend on, and which are simply general requirements for sustaining life?
- **Research link**: [NIDDK: Symptoms of Low Blood Glucose](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/diabetes/overview/preventing-problems/low-blood-glucose-hypoglycemia)

### 41. Glial Cells Participate in Neural Regulation

- **Scenario**: Alongside neuronal activity, surrounding glial cells take up substances, maintain the ionic environment, and regulate synapses.
- **Observed findings**: Astrocytes contribute to synapse formation and maintenance, as well as the regulation of neural activity.
- **Question to explore**: In trying to understand consciousness, are we overlooking physical dynamics beyond neurons?
- **Research link**: [2024 Review of Astrocytes and Synapses](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11293538/)

### 42. The Complexity of Brain Activity Changes with State

- **Scenario**: The same person's EEG perturbation responses differ between wakefulness and exposure to certain anesthetic drugs.
- **Observed findings**: Some EEG measures of criticality and perturbational complexity correlate with states of consciousness under anesthesia; this is insufficient to establish criticality as a universally necessary condition.
- **Question to explore**: What kind of dynamical structure is specifically associated with changes in consciousness?
- **Research link**: [2024 Study of Critical Dynamics in EEG](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11300875/)

## Ⅶ. Self-Awareness and the Internal Black Box (43–45)

> We can study our own thinking without having inherent access to all the physical processes that generate it.

### 43. Metacognition: Assessing Your Own Judgments

- **Scenario**: When answering a question, you not only provide an answer but also feel, “I am probably about 70% confident.”
- **Observed findings**: Subjective confidence and task accuracy can partly dissociate, and this relationship can be quantified experimentally.
- **Question to explore**: How does a system form a model of the reliability of its own cognition?
- **Research link**: [Neural Basis of Metacognitive Accuracy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3318765/)

### 44. Anosognosia

- **Scenario**: A stroke patient is paralyzed on one side of the body but insists that they can move normally.
- **Observed findings**: Certain brain injuries affect awareness of one's own impairments, rather than only damaging a particular motor ability.
- **Question to explore**: Why can self-knowledge become disconnected from the body's objective condition?
- **Research link**: [Systematic Review of Anosognosia for Hemiplegia](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12467583/)

### 45. Dissociation Between Body Ownership and Sense of Agency

- **Scenario**: This hand is mine, but the movement it just made did not feel like something “I decided to do.”
- **Observed findings**: The sense of body ownership and the sense of initiating an action are distinguishable dimensions of experience.
- **Question to explore**: Are “I own this body” and “I control this action” two different models of the self?
- **Research link**: [Review of Agency and Ownership](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5911504/)

## Ⅷ. Development, Evolution, and Different Substrates (46–48)

> Complex cognition may have multiple biological implementations, while subjective experience across species remains difficult to assess directly.

### 46. Development of Consciousness-Related Abilities in Infants

- **Scenario**: An infant cannot describe “what I am thinking right now,” yet gradually shows attention, learning, and complex perception.
- **Observed findings**: Neural and behavioral indicators in infants change during development, but criteria based on adult verbal reports cannot be applied directly.
- **Question to explore**: When does subjective experience emerge, and how can we avoid equating an inability to speak with an absence of consciousness?
- **Research link**: [2024 Review of Markers of Infant Consciousness](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11706756/)

### 47. Perception and Learning Across Species

- **Scenario**: Octopuses explore unfamiliar objects and crows solve complex tasks, but they cannot readily tell us “what it feels like.”
- **Observed findings**: Various birds, cephalopods, and other animals exhibit complex behavior and learning abilities; this alone does not directly establish phenomenal consciousness.
- **Question to explore**: What might different animals share in their experiences, and how might those experiences differ?
- **Research link**: [Review of Comparative Cognition in Cephalopods](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32893443/)

### 48. Different Neural Structures Implement Similar Functions

- **Scenario**: Birds and primates have markedly different brain organization, yet both can solve complex problems.
- **Observed findings**: Different evolutionary lineages have developed some similar cognitive abilities, showing functional convergence.
- **Question to explore**: Does consciousness require cortical structures unique to humans?
- **Research link**: [Convergent Evolution of Complex Brains and High Intelligence](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4650126/)

## Ⅸ. Acting Back on One's Own Neural Substrate (49–50)

> Cognition and scientific tools can, in turn, intervene in neural activity; this is a feedback relationship that can be studied.

### 49. Neurofeedback: Regulating Brain Activity Through Cognition

- **Scenario**: You see feedback about your own brain activity in real time and, with practice, learn to change the signal in a particular brain region.
- **Observed findings**: A systematic review reports measurable regulatory effects of insula neurofeedback training in some studies.
- **Question to explore**: To what extent can a system use feedback to change the substrate that produces its cognition?
- **Research link**: [2025 Systematic Review of Insula Neurofeedback](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41398-025-03382-8)

### 50. Electrical Brain Stimulation Alters Experience

- **Scenario**: During awake surgery, electrical stimulation of certain brain regions leads a patient to report sudden sounds, a sense of familiarity, or fragments that seem to come from past experiences.
- **Observed findings**: Local electrical stimulation can alter subjective content under specific conditions; apparent “recollections” may also contain constructed or misattributed elements.
- **Question to explore**: What causal mapping connects changes in physical activity to changes in subjective content?
- **Research link**: [Brain Stimulation and Elicited Memories](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10542740/)

## Research Boundaries and Reference Framework

As of October 2026, there is still no universally accepted unified theory of the necessary and sufficient conditions for subjective experience. A 2025 preregistered adversarial collaboration study directly comparing integrated information theory and global neuronal workspace theory both supported and challenged key predictions of the two theories. Article page: [Nature: Adversarial Testing of Global Neuronal Workspace and Integrated Information Theories of Consciousness](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08888-1).

> **The goal of this repository is not to prove a particular theory of consciousness, but to ensure that every hypothesis is constrained by clear observations, interventions, and counterexamples.**
