# 1. Model Assumptions

- **p1.** See the fundamental assumption: human consciousness depends on ongoing neurophysiological activity and its particular organization. Changes in the organization of this activity may alter the level and content of consciousness.
- **p2.** Consciousness is related to the system's continuous updating of its internal state. External stimuli, signals from within the body, and spontaneous neural activity jointly influence conscious content and states.
- **p3.** Memory and consciousness are deeply coupled. Memory encoding, activation, and reconstruction are closely connected to the formation of conscious content. However, intact long-term memory is not a necessary condition for every individual moment of consciousness.
- **p4.** The system that supports consciousness has a hierarchical architecture. The human nervous system contains multiple interacting levels of processing, some of which operate without subjective awareness. Conscious cognition, learning, and action can, in turn, modify these neural mechanisms through feedback. Whether the specific conditions for consciousness depend on a particular form of hierarchical organization remains to be tested.
- **p5.** Emotion is an important manifestation of, or influence on, consciousness. Emotional experience is deeply related to internal bodily states, neuromodulation, and cognitive appraisal. Ongoing feedback between the body and brain may offer an important route toward understanding subjective feelings, but whether emotion is a necessary foundation for all consciousness remains unresolved. The term "emotion" is somewhat abstract, whereas "feeling" is too broad to describe the intended concept precisely.
- **p6.** To be added.

# 2. Further Analysis of Relevant Phenomena and Associated Hypotheses

## Phenomenon 1: People are conscious while dreaming, with consciousness operating through a simulation of reality. In the absence of disturbances such as sensory stimulation, external sensory information does not actively enter subjective experience as it does during wakefulness.

1. **Siclari et al. (2017)**  
   *The neural correlates of dreaming*  
   Nature Neuroscience  
   https://www.nature.com/articles/nn.4545

2. **Salvesen et al. (2024)**  
   *Influencing dreams through sensory stimulation: A systematic review*  
   Sleep Medicine Reviews  
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11009489/

**Analysis:** Dreams can include visual, auditory, emotional, and self-related experiences, even when the individual is not maintaining normal contact with the external environment as during wakefulness.

Using high-density EEG recordings, Siclari and colleagues found that dream experience was associated with particular patterns of cortical activity in both REM and NREM sleep.

This indicates that internal neural activity can support subjective experience when external sensory input is substantially reduced.

However, external sensory input is not entirely unable to influence dreams. Sounds, touch, and other stimuli during sleep can sometimes become incorporated into dream content.

## Phenomenon 2: Consciousness seems to have only one focus of attention at any given moment.

1. **Pashler (1994)**  
   *Dual-task interference in simple tasks: Data and theory*  
   Psychological Bulletin  
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7972591/

2. **Consciousness and Attention: On Sufficiency and Necessity (2011)**  
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3153822/

**Analysis:** People can process large amounts of information simultaneously, yet find it difficult to perform several tasks at once when each requires substantial deliberate control.

Classic dual-task experiments have found that a processing bottleneck often arises when both tasks require response selection: one task delays the response to the other.

This supports a mechanism in which central cognitive control has limited capacity, while many peripheral information-processing operations can run in parallel.

## Phenomenon 3: Through learning, skills can gradually become automatic, reducing the need for sustained deliberate attention and executive control.

1. **Schneider & Shiffrin (1977)**  
   *Controlled and Automatic Human Information Processing I*  
   Psychological Review  
   https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.84.1.1

2. **Grillner (2025)**  
   *How circuits for habits are formed within the basal ganglia*  
   PNAS  
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12002322/

**Analysis:** Schneider and Shiffrin's classic research distinguished controlled processing from automatic processing.

With sustained practice, some tasks can become less dependent on limited attentional resources.

Neuroscience research has further found that skill learning may change the neural control mechanisms on which task execution depends.

For example, in certain animal motor experiments, learning some action sequences initially requires cortical involvement, whereas execution after extensive training can rely more heavily on the relevant basal ganglia circuits.

This suggests that learning does more than store knowledge: it may also change the system's internal organization of control.

## Phenomenon 4: Specialized neural processing systems have internal operations that are not fully accessible to subjective awareness.

1. **Crump & Logan (2010)**  
   *Hierarchical control and skilled typing*  
   Journal of Experimental Psychology  
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20919783/

2. **Mashour et al. (2020)**  
   *Conscious Processing and the Global Neuronal Workspace Hypothesis*  
   Neuron  
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32135090/

**Analysis:** Research on skilled typing has proposed a hierarchical feedback control model:

- A higher level handles word-level goals.
- A lower level executes individual keystrokes.
- Feedback coordinates the different levels of control.
- The higher level does not need access to every detail of lower-level execution.

## Phenomenon 5: A person can behave in ways consistent with their long-term preferences and experience without explicitly bringing all relevant preferences, memories, and action decisions into conscious thought at the moment of acting.

1. **Leveraging cognitive neuroscience for making and breaking real-world habits (2025)**  
   Trends in Cognitive Sciences  
   https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1364661324002663

2. **Bouton (2024)**  
   *Habit and persistence*  
   Journal of the Experimental Analysis of Behavior  
   https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jeab.894

**Analysis:** Cognitive neuroscience commonly distinguishes goal-directed control from habitual control.

**Goal-directed control:**

Action selection is influenced by anticipated outcomes and their current value.

**Habitual control:**

Certain learned stimulus–response associations can directly trigger behavior in particular contexts.

Researchers often distinguish the two through outcome-devaluation experiments.

If an individual continues performing an action after the outcome it originally pursued has lost its value, this may indicate a greater contribution from habitual control.

However, habitual behavior does not imply a complete absence of conscious involvement. Goal-directed and habitual processing can interact.

## Phenomenon 6: Sustaining a research goal and continuously retaining all intermediate information are two different capacities.

1. **Paluch et al. (2025)**  
   *Unattended working memory items are coded by persistent activity in human medial temporal lobe neurons*  
   Nature Human Behaviour  
   https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02235-0

2. **Koechlin et al. (2003)**  
   *The architecture of cognitive control in the human prefrontal cortex*  
   Science  
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14615530/

**Analysis:** Research on working memory and cognitive control indicates that:

- The current focus of attention has limited capacity.
- Information does not necessarily disappear as soon as it leaves the focus of attention.
- Higher-level task goals can continue to influence behavior as local information changes.

A 2025 study recording human neuronal activity found that some working memory content outside the current focus of attention could still be represented through persistent neural activity.

Research on prefrontal cognitive control also indicates that task goals operating at different timescales can be organized through hierarchical control mechanisms.

Therefore, the information currently in the focus of consciousness is not equivalent to all the information the system is retaining and using.

## Phenomenon 7: If not everyone experiences an inner voice or visual mental imagery, might conscious thought involve more fundamental representations that do not depend on a particular sensory modality?

1. **Nedergaard & Lupyan (2024)**  
   *Not Everybody Has an Inner Voice*  
   Psychological Science  
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38728320/

2. **Zeman et al. (2015)**  
   *Lives without imagery: Congenital aphantasia*  
   Cortex  
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26115582/

3. **Lind (2025)**  
   *Are There Really People With No Inner Voice?*  
   Psychological Science  
   https://doi.org/10.1177/09567976251335583

**Analysis:** Research on inner speech in 2024 found substantial differences in how frequently people reported experiencing an inner voice.

Participants who reported less inner speech showed differences in performance on some verbal working memory and rhyme-judgment tasks.

Research on aphantasia has found that some individuals have difficulty voluntarily forming visual mental images, yet can still perform many cognitive tasks.

However, the claim that inner speech can be entirely absent remains disputed.

In 2025, a researcher argued that existing evidence supports individual differences in the frequency of inner speech, but does not yet rigorously establish that some people have no inner speech at all.

These studies suggest that different people may rely on different forms of internal representation when thinking.

## Phenomenon 8: The continuity of consciousness does not necessarily mean that conscious content remains unchanged; maintaining a goal does not mean continuously attending to the same item of information.

1. **Koechlin et al. (2003)**  
   *The architecture of cognitive control in the human prefrontal cortex*  
   Science  
   https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1088545

2. **Logan & Crump (2011)**  
   *Hierarchical Control of Cognitive Processes: The Case for Skilled Typewriting*  
   https://doi.org/10.1016/B978-0-12-385527-5.00001-2

**Analysis:** Research on cognitive control indicates that the brain can organize tasks across different timescales.

Koechlin and colleagues proposed that control processes in the human prefrontal cortex have a degree of hierarchical organization.

Research on skilled typing likewise proposes that a higher level can maintain word-level goals while a lower level continually executes individual keystrokes.

Thus, a change in the content of local processing does not require a corresponding change in the overall goal.

However, persistence of a goal cannot be directly equated with the continuity of phenomenal consciousness.

## Phenomenon 9: If automatic mechanisms are an important basis for the efficient functioning of subjective consciousness, can human learning be understood as a process that continually transforms complex tasks initially requiring deliberate control into internal capabilities that can later be accessed at low cost? The efficiency of conscious functioning may arise not from an exceptionally powerful capacity for immediate computation within subjective consciousness itself, but from an extensive foundation of nonconscious processing, built through long-term learning throughout the nervous system, that can be flexibly accessed and recombined.

1. **Thalmann, Souza & Oberauer (2019)**  
   *How does chunking help working memory?*  
   Journal of Experimental Psychology  
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29698045/

2. **Solopchuk et al. (2016)**  
   *Chunking improves symbolic sequence processing and relies on working memory gating mechanisms*  
   Learning & Memory  
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4755266/

3. **Speed-accuracy trade-off in skilled typewriting (2013)**  
   *Decomposing the contributions of hierarchical control loops*  
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23127474/

4. **Leveraging cognitive neuroscience for making and breaking real-world habits (2025)**  
   Trends in Cognitive Sciences  
   https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1364661324002663

**Analysis:** What we should investigate is not the upper limit of conscious capacity, but how the extensive nonconscious system supporting subjective consciousness reduces the cost of deliberate thought through learning, accumulation, and automatization, including chunking. This may involve a more general process: numerous complex, repetitive cognitive operations gradually form efficient internal structures, allowing new thought to build on structures already in place. Familiar situations are handled by learned mechanisms; deliberate control increases when conflicts or anomalies arise, or when a flexible change of strategy is needed.

**Deliberate control and automatic processing cooperate dynamically.**

Many skilled operations do not require continuous supervision, allowing limited resources for deliberate control to be devoted more fully to new problems, complex reasoning, and strategic adjustment.

At the same time, further learning may gradually make some complex operations automatic.

This creates a cycle:

New tasks → Deliberate learning → Repeated practice → Chunking and automatization → Lower costs of deliberate control → Support for more complex tasks

It is important to emphasize that learning processes differ across tasks: not all cognitive skills can be assumed to follow the same path toward automatization.
